## Findings

- **[bug: wrong measurement] bench.sh:129-143 with 02_walk_ltree.sql:32 and 03_walk_arrays.sql:49.** The timed interval runs from `SELECT clock_timestamp() AS t0 \gset` to the `clock_timestamp()` inside the recording INSERT. In between, the walk file runs its dispatch query (`SELECT :'method' = 'L1' AS is_l1 \gset` in 02, `SELECT ... AS is_pred, ... AS is_1km \gset` in 03), which is a full client-server round trip plus parse and plan. 04_walk_pointer.sql has no dispatch query. So every L1, L2, A-pred and A timing carries one more round trip than P's. The t0 result travelling back and the INSERT travelling out add about one more round trip to every method. On Docker Desktop's port forwarding that is a fraction of a millisecond, and the single-origin cells are about that size: EXPLAIN gives A 0.43 ms and P 3.9 ms for one origin, and the L2 per-origin reps in `logs/cell_L2_full_1_default_*` are 2.2 to 4 ms. So the 1-origin A-against-P comparison is biased against A by a constant that does not belong to either method. Fix: compute the dispatch booleans in `cell_script` (`\set is_l1 ...` from the bash `$method`/`$walk`), or take t0 immediately before the walk statement. Then time an empty origin set (`lo` > `hi`) once per method to measure the residual overhead and report it.

- **[fragile: pooled measurements] 06_timings.sql:16 (`CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS fwapg.bench_timings`) and 08_summary.sql:23-52.** `setup` drops and rebuilds `bench_cells` and `bench_out` but never empties `bench_timings`. The summary groups by (method, walk, cell, settings) with no run or time filter, so rows from any earlier `bench.sh cell` or `timings` run are pooled into the medians and notes. That would include runs under the abandoned INSERT sink. The setup logs show three setups (16:38, 16:41, 17:15Z), and `setup_20261021T171502Z.log` says "relation bench_timings already exists, skipping". The one committed earlier cell log (`cell_P_full_1_default_20261021T163808Z.log`) failed before it timed anything, so the table is probably clean. Nothing guarantees it, though. Before `summary`, check `SELECT min(at), count(*) FILTER (WHERE at < '2026-10-21 17:15:46+00') FROM fwapg.bench_timings`. Make `setup` truncate the table, or have the summary filter on a run start.

- **[fragile: measurement differs from plan] 06_timings.sql:49-56 with 08_summary.sql:55-62, 84.** The L1 1,000 cell and the L2 "budget" cells run once with no warm-up because they were expected to be slow (L1 "about 1 s an origin" in 07_results.sql:5; L2 "40 min to 3.6 h" in the plan). The summary projects a cell only when it has no timed repeat. The measured rates say these cells will mostly finish:
  - L1 single origins take 25 to 36 ms (`logs/cell_L1_full_1_default_*`), so the 1,000 cell takes about 30 to 40 s.
  - L2 runs at 12.6 ms an origin at 1,000 origins, so random 100,000 takes about 21 min, under the 30 min timeout, and BULK's 30,046 origins take about 6 min.
  
  A cell that finishes reports **one unwarmed repeat** as its median. The other methods' cells report the median of three warmed repeats. L1 1,000 runs first in its cell group (`run_order` sorts by cell, then method), so it is the first statement to read `fwa_streams` for origins 101..1,000. The plan says "L1 at 1,000 is one repeat, projected from the 100 cell's three", but the summary will report a cold single run as a measurement instead. Also, L2 100,000 may finish at defaults and time out at noparallel, which puts the two settings on different footing. Either give these cells a warm-up and three repeats now that they fit, or always project them and print the single run beside the projection.

- **[bug: attribution can pass vacuously] 07_compare.sql:202-226 (`route_differs`).** The predicate fires for every unclassified A-P difference of an origin as soon as **any** difference of that origin lies on a blue line the other method never visits. It never tests the stated cause: the tree and the paths taking different lines and the chains coming back together. The case that matters is a pointer walk that stops early (a missing `downstream_id`, which is the risk with the 27,096 in-tree nulls and the 383 dead ends) or an array walk that stops early. Every row the longer walk has on the following lines is on a line the shorter walk never visits, so **all** of the origin's differences become `route_differs`, and "assert: no unattributed difference" passes. The second rule (221-226) widens this for 1 km: any classified full-walk difference anywhere on the origin's chain, even tens of km down, attributes every 1 km difference at that origin, including a defect in A's 1 km bounding. Today the class holds 25 segments at 4 origins (full) and 16 at 3 (1 km), so check those 7 origins by hand. Then tighten the predicate: require both chains to end at the same final segment (a rejoin), or both to end at a root or path end, and require the 1 km rule's cause to lie within the first 1,000 m.

- **[fragile: class defined by the method under test] 07_compare.sql:250-256 (`side_channel`, 1,661,801 segments).** The class is "L2 has the segment and its line is not in `cmp_lines`", and `cmp_lines` is built from `fwapg.blk_paths`, the same source the array walk reads. So if the paths left out a line the water does pass, L2's rows on it are filed as side channels. The only independent check of the paths is the A-P comparison, which has the `route_differs` catch-all above. Nothing in the predicate tests that the segment is a side channel. The research draft labels these "side channels, lines the water does not pass through". A cheap discriminator: count the class's rows with `x.is_main` (`blue_line_key = watershed_key`). Main-stem rows in a "side channel" class would be suspect. Report that count, or require `NOT x.is_main` and leave the rest unattributed. The same origin-wide shape is in the L2-A `localcode_anomaly` origin clause (304-313), which attributes every remaining difference at an origin whose own local code is anomalous.

- **[fragile] 07_compare.sql:375-399, order classes not scoped by pair.** `code_tie_across_lines`, `localcode_anomaly` (scoped only `!= 'L1-L2'`) and `path_through_side_channel` (any side-channel segment involved) apply to the A-P order differences too. Neither A nor P reads codes. A-P has no order differences today, but a future one would be attributed to a code cause instead of reaching `unattributed`. Scope these updates to the pairs that involve L1 or L2.

- **[doc claim not backed by the logged run] research/fwa_downstream_queries.md:40 ("they agree on every distance to the bit") and :18; findings.md:128 ("the largest difference there is 0").** 07_compare.sql:129-141 only inserts distance differences over 0.01 m, and the log prints only the 1 cm assertion. No logged output carries a maximum. The two walks also apply the expression at different places. A applies the junction `CASE` to **every** segment of an entered line (03_walk_arrays.sql:111-112, 159-160). P applies it to the entry segment only, and uses `length_metre` below (01_downstream_ids.sql:105, 110). The two agree only while `length_metre` is no more than about 1 cm over that segment's measure span. In this schema the span is `ST_Length(geom)`, which is not `length_metre`. Print `max(abs(a.cum_m - b.cum_m))` in 07_compare, or say "within 1 cm" in the research file.

## Checked and sound (no finding)

- Heredoc generator: `\\` to `\`, `$method`/`$r`/`${BENCH_TIMEOUT}` expand, and nothing else in either heredoc has `$` or backticks. The `printf '\\set ...'` lines are right. `:'LAST_ERROR_MESSAGE'` is quoted safely. `:origin_rank` gives NULL in SQL and 'NULL' in the text.
- `\if :ERROR` reads the walk statement's status, and a timeout records `57014` as `timeout` and then `\q`. `SET statement_timeout` and the noparallel `SET` precede the repeats. The warm-up is rep 0 and the summary excludes it. The per-origin cells give each origin its own warm-up.
- `timings` loop: no command inside it reads the loop's stdin (`run_psql -f ... < /dev/null`, `-c` calls, piped `-f -`).
- `qa_check`: a missing line, a NULL or an `f` fails it, and under `pipefail` a run with no `assert:` lines also fails the step.
- `\set sink_open 'INSERT INTO ' :out ' (...)'` concatenates as intended, and `:out` stays constant within each of 07_results and 07_compare, so caching it at the first include is harmless.
- A 1 km bound: lines and segments it skips are always at least 1,000 m from the origin (a line's lower cut forces the next line's `entry_m` over 1,001). A negative `junction - bottom` (junction below a parent's first segment) only makes the bound read more. `cum_m - step_m < 1000` matches P's `w.cum_m < 1000`.
- downstream_ids: node keys match `qa_topology.sql` exactly, and the primary keys make each rule's target unique. Rule 3 uses A's `drm < junction - 0.001` and the same `step_m` `CASE`. The QA reverse walk is a sound acyclic-and-complete test for a graph with one pointer per segment. `step_m > 0` holds by construction (`> 0.001` on a junction step).
- L2 calls `fwa_downstream` in the right direction (a = origin, b = segment, 8-argument form). A-pred's argument order matches `blk_on_or_upstream`'s "b on or upstream of a". `00_paths_md5.sql` orders by a unique key.
- 08_summary: the medians exclude the warm-up and NULLs. Per-origin cells take the median of three per origin, then the median and p95 across origins. The projection arithmetic (ms / origins × origins / 1000 = s) is right.
